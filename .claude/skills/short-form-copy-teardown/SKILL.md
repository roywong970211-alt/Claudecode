# Short-Form Copy Teardown

Reverse-engineer the copywriting of a short-form video (Instagram Reel, YouTube
Short, TikTok) and name exactly what the creator did right — so the patterns can
be reused. This skill is **analysis**, not scriptwriting. It explains *why* the
piece works across four pillars: **Title/Caption**, **Cover (Thumbnail)**,
**Psychology**, and **Hook & Language**.

## When to use

- The user shares a Reel/Short/TikTok and asks why it works, what's good about
  it, or to "break it down."
- The user wants to extract a repeatable template from a winning video.
- The user wants their own draft title/cover/hook graded against proven patterns.

## Inputs you need (and how to get them)

You cannot analyze what you cannot see. Instagram/TikTok/YouTube pages are
login-walled and usually blocked from automated fetching, so a bare link is
rarely enough. Before analyzing, make sure you have as many of these as possible:

1. **The spoken script / transcript** — word for word if possible.
2. **On-screen text** — captions, callouts, the words burned into the video.
3. **The title / caption** — the written post text and the first line especially.
4. **The cover / thumbnail** — the still frame + any text overlaid on it.
5. **Context** — creator's niche, follower count if known, the video's
   performance (views/saves/shares) if known, and the platform.

If any are missing, **ask the user to paste the transcript, the caption text,
and a screenshot or description of the cover.** Do not guess the content of a
video you have not seen. If the user can only give a link, tell them the link
alone can't be opened and request the text/screenshots instead. Never fabricate
a script, caption, or on-screen text.

## Workflow

1. **Gather & restate.** Collect the inputs above. Restate the raw material you
   were given (transcript, caption, cover text) so the user can confirm it's
   right before you analyze it.
2. **Identify the format.** Name the content archetype (e.g. listicle, "I tried
   X for N days", contrarian take, tutorial, story/transformation, myth-bust,
   POV). Format sets expectations for everything else.
3. **Score each pillar** using the checklists below. For every item, quote the
   exact words the creator used and state the mechanism — *what* they did and
   *why it works on the viewer.*
4. **Extract the transferable pattern.** Turn each win into a reusable template
   with a fill-in-the-blank formula.
5. **Flag what's missing or weak** (optional but valuable) — the one or two
   things that, if the user replicates this, they should strengthen.
6. **Deliver** using the output template.

## Pillar 1 — Title / Caption copywriting

What to look for and credit when present:

- **First-3-words rule.** The opening words carry the click; feed UIs truncate.
  Quote them and judge whether they create a reason to tap "more."
- **Specificity & numbers.** Concrete numbers, timeframes, and nouns
  ("$4,000", "in 30 days", "3 mistakes") out-convert vague claims.
- **Open loop / curiosity gap.** A promise whose payoff is withheld until the
  watch.
- **Named stakes or outcome.** A clear benefit ("so you never…") or cost
  avoided ("stop doing X").
- **Target call-out.** Naming the exact audience ("if you're a beginner lifter…")
  so the right viewer self-selects.
- **Readability.** Short clauses, plain words, line breaks, strategic emoji as
  visual anchors (not decoration).
- **CTA alignment.** Does the caption drive the action the creator wants
  (save/share/comment/follow) and is it matched to the content's value?

## Pillar 2 — Cover / Thumbnail

- **Legibility at thumbnail size.** 3–5 words max, high contrast, big type that
  survives a small feed tile.
- **Text ≠ title.** The best covers say something *different* from the caption
  to widen the curiosity gap, not repeat it.
- **Face / emotion / gaze.** Human face, strong expression, or eye direction
  pulling attention to the text.
- **Visual contradiction or pattern interrupt.** Something that doesn't belong,
  forcing a second look.
- **Promise made visual.** The transformation, result, or "before/after" shown,
  not just told.
- **Brand consistency.** Recurring color, font, or framing that makes the
  creator's covers recognizable across a feed.

## Pillar 3 — Psychology

Name the cognitive levers at work. Credit any that apply:

- **Curiosity gap** — an unresolved question the brain wants closed.
- **Pattern interrupt** — breaking the expected to reset attention.
- **Specificity bias** — precise numbers feel more credible than round ones.
- **Loss aversion** — "stop losing / avoid this mistake" beats equivalent gains.
- **Social proof / authority** — results, credentials, "everyone's doing X."
- **Identity & in-group** — "people like you," insider language, us-vs-them.
- **Zeigarnik effect / open loops** — unfinished stories hold attention.
- **Cognitive ease** — simple words and short sentences lower the cost of
  watching, so people keep watching.
- **Reciprocity / value-first** — giving a genuinely useful tip earns the
  save/follow.
- **Emotional activation** — surprise, relatability, mild outrage, aspiration.

For each lever, point to the exact line or frame that triggers it.

## Pillar 4 — Hook & Language (the first 1–3 seconds + word choice)

The hook is where retention is won or lost. Analyze:

- **Hook type** — question, bold claim, negative/contrarian, result-first,
  story cold-open, "POV", direct call-out, "nobody talks about…".
- **Time-to-value** — how fast the first payoff lands; stalling kills retention.
- **Spoken vs. on-screen hook** — do the audio and the text hook reinforce each
  other or cover two different hooks for two viewer types?
- **Natural, conversational language** — contractions, second person ("you"),
  spoken rhythm, no corporate phrasing. Verbatim, scroll-stopping language
  feels like a person talking, not a brand broadcasting.
- **Sensory & concrete nouns/verbs** over abstractions.
- **Rhythm & pacing devices** — short punchy sentences, rule of three, repetition,
  a mid-video "re-hook" that restarts attention.
- **Retention engineering** — open loops opened early and paid off late,
  "wait for it," list countdowns, cliffhanger before a cut.
- **Payoff & CTA** — the ending delivers on the hook's promise and tells the
  viewer what to do next.

## Rules

- **Quote, don't paraphrase.** Every claim about the copy must be anchored to the
  creator's actual words or a described frame.
- **Mechanism over praise.** "This is a great hook" is useless; name the lever
  and why it moves the viewer.
- **No fabrication.** If you don't have the transcript, caption, or cover, say so
  and ask for it. Never invent what the video said.
- **Keep it transferable.** The point is a pattern the user can reuse, not just a
  compliment to the original creator.
